About this school

Tasmania's oldest school, with boarding and co-education since the 1970s

For international families: This profile is designed to help overseas families evaluate Launceston Church Grammar School. It covers CRICOS registration, visa requirements, English language prerequisites, and the international application process. If you are an Australian resident, please visit the school's enrolments page for domestic admissions.

Launceston Church Grammar School, commonly known as Launceston Grammar, opened its doors on 15 June 1846 with 24 boys enrolled under the headmastership of Rev Henry Kane. It holds the distinction of being Australia's oldest continuously operating independent school, a heritage spanning 180 years. The school is an Anglican co-educational day and boarding community that today enrols approximately 700 students from Early Learning through to Grade 12 across two campuses in Launceston, northern Tasmania.

The school became co-educational in the 1970s and amalgamated with Broadland House Girls' School in 1982, which became the Junior Campus site. Today, the Junior Campus at East Launceston serves students from Early Learning to Grade 6 and operates the IB Primary Years Programme. The Senior Campus at Mowbray hosts Grades 7 to 12 and includes Hawkes Boarding, with separate houses for boys and girls. Launceston Grammar is a member of the Australian Boarding Schools' Association (ABSA) and is affiliated with Independent Schools Tasmania.

International families should note that Launceston Grammar is registered on CRICOS (provider code 00650K) and accepts international students in Grades 7 to 12. The school has a dedicated International Student Coordinator, provides a personalised English as an Additional Language programme, and works with a published list of approved education agents. International boarders come from across South-East Asia, including Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Hong Kong and China.

Academics

Curriculum and academic pathways

TCE IB PYP (Junior Campus) Aviation Agricultural Science Outdoor Education

International students at Launceston Grammar follow the Tasmanian Certificate of Education (TCE), the state's senior secondary credential. The school offers an extensive number of TCE subjects, and its academic structure is organised into three stages: Middle Grammar (Grades 7 to 9), which focuses on broad exploration and foundational skills; and Grammar Grads (Grades 10 to 12), where students specialise and prepare for tertiary pathways. The Junior Campus runs the IB Primary Years Programme for Early Learning to Grade 6, though this is outside the scope of CRICOS registration for international students.

What sets Launceston Grammar apart from many Australian schools is its range of specialist programmes. The school was only the second in Australia to introduce an outdoor education programme, which remains a core part of the student experience. Other distinctive offerings include aviation studies, agricultural science, mountain biking on a purpose-built 2 km campus track, and photography. A Learning Enrichment team supports students with individual learning needs, while extension programmes are available for academically advanced students.

International admissions

How to apply as an international student

Launceston Grammar is registered under the ESOS Act 2000 to enrol international students on a Student Visa (subclass 500). The school offers two CRICOS-registered courses covering Grades 7 to 12. Applications can be made directly through the school's enrolments office or via one of the school's approved education agents. The school publishes a current agent list on its international students page.

Contact the enrolments office or an approved agent. Email enrolments@lcgs.tas.edu.au to request an Application for Enrolment form. You can also work with one of the school's listed education agents.
Prepare English proficiency evidence. The school accepts several forms: AEAS results with stanines of 6 or above in non-verbal ability and mathematics reasoning; a report from an approved ELICOS provider; demonstrated proficiency during an enrolment interview; or evidence of three or more years of schooling delivered in English.
Gather supporting documents. You will need: a copy of the student's passport, academic transcripts and reports from the current school, evidence of English language proficiency, and any other assessments requested by the school.
Submit the application. Complete and return the Application for Enrolment form along with the $110 online application fee. Note: lodging the form is not an offer of enrolment or notice of acceptance.
Application assessment. The school evaluates academic reports and English proficiency. The Director of Learning makes the final decision on acceptance.
Receive a Letter of Offer. If successful, the Principal issues a Letter of Offer in writing, accompanied by a fee invoice.
Accept and pay advance fees. Complete the acceptance forms and pay the advance fee: the $2,000 non-refundable capital fee, plus 50% of annual tuition, levy and boarding fees.
Receive your eCoE and apply for your visa. Upon receipt of acceptance and payment, the school issues an electronic Confirmation of Enrolment (eCoE). Use this to lodge your Student Visa (subclass 500) application with the Department of Home Affairs. You will need Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) and must meet the Genuine Student Test.

Boarding

Hawkes Boarding at Launceston Grammar

Boarding has been part of Launceston Grammar for much of its 180-year history. Hawkes Boarding is located at the centre of the Senior Campus in Mowbray, with separate houses for boys and girls. Each boarding house has its own lounge area, student kitchen and dedicated study spaces. Boarders have access to the school's full range of facilities, including playing ovals, a gymnasium, indoor swimming pool, cricket nets and a purpose-built 2 km mountain bike track. The school draws domestic boarders primarily from regional and rural Tasmania, while international boarders come from across South-East Asia and China.

Co-curricular

Sport, arts and activities

Launceston Grammar requires all students to participate in at least one area of its co-curricular programme. The school competes in inter-school sport across northern Tasmania and runs the Duke of Edinburgh Award scheme. Its outdoor education programme, one of the earliest in Australia, takes advantage of the Tasmanian landscape with expeditions and camps built into the curriculum. The performing arts programme includes choirs, ensembles, drama productions and soiree performances, while community service opportunities operate both within school hours and beyond.

Location

Living in Launceston, Tasmania

Launceston is a regional city of around 72,000 people in northern Tasmania. It is one of Australia's oldest cities, founded in 1806, and is known for its well-preserved heritage architecture, parks, restaurants and cultural precincts. For international families, Launceston offers a smaller, safer environment compared to major capital cities, with strong community connections. The city is compact and walkable, making it easy for students to navigate independently.

International students flying into Australia will typically arrive at Melbourne or Sydney before connecting on a short domestic flight (45 to 90 minutes) to Launceston Airport (LST), which is 15 km from the city centre. The school offers a chaperone service to meet students at their arrival airport and assist with the domestic connection. Launceston General Hospital, a major public hospital serving northern Tasmania, is located nearby. The Senior Campus at Mowbray is accessible by local bus services, though most boarders travel by school transport or on foot.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What subjects and curriculum does Launceston Grammar offer for international students?
International students at Launceston Grammar follow the Tasmanian Certificate of Education (TCE) pathway. The school offers an extensive range of TCE subjects across Grades 7 to 12, including specialist programmes in aviation, agricultural science, outdoor education, and mountain biking. The Junior Campus runs the IB Primary Years Programme, though this is not available to international students as CRICOS registration covers Grades 7 to 12 only.
How much are the school fees at Launceston Grammar for international students in 2026?
International tuition fees range from $29,770/year for Grades 7 to 8, up to $31,975/year for Grades 11 to 12. Boarding costs $26,735/year across all grade levels. An annual levy of $7,440 to $7,995 applies on top of tuition. Total annual costs including tuition, levy and boarding range from $63,945 to $66,705 depending on grade level. A one-off $2,000 capital fee and $110 application fee also apply.
Can international students apply to Launceston Grammar from overseas?
Yes. Launceston Church Grammar School is registered on CRICOS (code 00650K) and accepts international students in Grades 7 to 12. Applicants need to demonstrate adequate English proficiency and can apply through the school's enrolments office or via one of the school's approved education agents. The school maintains a published list of approved agents on its website.
Is boarding available at Launceston Grammar for international students?
Yes. Hawkes Boarding provides accommodation from Grade 5 onward, though international students are accepted from Grade 7. Male and female students are housed in separate boarding houses on the Senior Campus. Each house has lounge areas, student kitchens and study spaces. Facilities include a gymnasium, indoor swimming pool, cricket nets, and a purpose-built 2 km mountain bike track. Supervised prep sessions run Monday to Thursday evenings.
What English proficiency test do I need to get into Launceston Grammar?
The school accepts several forms of evidence: AEAS test results with stanines of 6 or above in non-verbal ability and mathematics reasoning, completion of an approved ELICOS programme, demonstrated proficiency during an enrolment interview, or evidence of three or more years of schooling delivered entirely in English. Students may be required to complete intensive English study before entering mainstream classes.
What sports and activities are available at Launceston Grammar?
The school offers a wide range of co-curricular activities. Sports include rowing, cricket, football, soccer, tennis, swimming, athletics, cross country, mountain biking and table tennis. Other programmes include the Duke of Edinburgh Award, debating and public speaking, music ensembles and choirs, drama, outdoor education expeditions, and community service. All students are expected to participate in at least one area of the co-curricular programme.
What visa does my child need to study at Launceston Grammar in Australia?
International students studying full-time require a Student Visa (subclass 500). After receiving a Confirmation of Enrolment (eCoE) from the school, you apply through the Department of Home Affairs. Students under 18 must have approved welfare arrangements; boarders are covered by the school's boarding arrangement. Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is mandatory for the duration of the visa.
Does Launceston Grammar work with education agents?
Yes. The school maintains a published list of approved education agents who can assist international families with the application process. The agent list is available for download on the school's international students page. Families can also apply directly by contacting the enrolments office at enrolments@lcgs.tas.edu.au.
